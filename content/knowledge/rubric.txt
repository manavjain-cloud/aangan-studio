AANGAN STUDIO — ACCEPT / REJECT RUBRIC
Lead Qualification, Importance Scores, Nine Checks, and Workflow Actions
Version: 1.0
Scope: Incoming phone calls only

PURPOSE
This rubric defines how the AI Voice Agent should qualify incoming phone enquiries, when it should accept,
clarify, reject, or escalate a call, and how the result should flow into the four downstream outputs:
1. HubSpot
2. Email notification
3. Calendly booking
4. Management dashboard

The rubric is based on the supplied MESA Case 03 brief, services.md, qualified.md, pricing.md, the
September 2026 enquiry transcripts, the latest Components Map, and the Nine Checks framework.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
There are two different rubrics in this document:
A. Lead qualification: decides whether an enquiry should be passed to a designer.
B. Nine Checks: decides whether the automation itself deserves to be built and operated.
Do not use the Nine Checks score to qualify individual customers, and do not use a lead score to override
a clear service, scope, or timeline exclusion.

===============================================================================
PART A — LEAD ACCEPTANCE / REJECTION RUBRIC
===============================================================================

A1. WEIGHTED IMPORTANCE SCORE (100 POINTS)

The source documents define five qualification criteria but do not assign numeric weights. The weights
below are PROPOSED implementation weights, based on the rubric's ordering and the importance of each
criterion to service fit. They should be approved by Nikhil before production use.

Criterion 1 — Real project with design AND execution
Importance: 30 / 100
PASS (+30):
- The caller wants Aangan to provide design and execution, not just advice.
- Eligible examples include a residential full-home project, a qualifying partial-home project, or a
  complete single-room redesign with materials, furniture, and execution.
- Commercial projects may qualify when they fit Aangan's office / clinic / studio scope and size.
FAIL (0):
- Advice-only, styling-only, a second opinion, or the caller will handle execution themselves.
- Standalone furniture sourcing or standalone Vastu advice.
- Retail, hospitality, restaurants, hotels, or gyms.
CLARIFY:
- If it is unclear whether execution is required, ask one direct question:
  "Are you looking for full design and execution, or design advice only?"
Notes:
- A single-room project is not automatically disqualified if full execution is included.
- A rented apartment is acceptable if the scope is suitable and no structural changes are required.

Criterion 2 — Inside Aangan's service area
Importance: 25 / 100
PASS (+25):
- The project is in Pune city or PCMC, within the areas served by Aangan.
FAIL (0):
- The project is outside the service area, including Nashik, Talegaon, Lonavala, Mumbai, or other
  unserved locations.
CLARIFY:
- If the location is missing or ambiguous, ask:
  "Which locality is the property in?"
Notes:
- Do not make an exception based on project size, referral, or willingness to pay if Aangan cannot
  serve the location with its current vendor network.

Criterion 3 — Realistic timeline and site readiness
Importance: 20 / 100
PASS (+20):
- The required completion date is feasible for the described scope and the site will be available in time.
- Use Aangan's stated design and execution durations to assess feasibility. The services guide gives a
  3–4 week design phase and 8–16 weeks of execution, depending on project size and site readiness.
FAIL (0):
- The caller requires completion on a clearly impossible timeline, such as a three-week turnaround
  for a project that requires design and execution.
CLARIFY:
- If the deadline or site availability is unclear, ask:
  "When would you need the project completed, and when will the site be available?"
Notes:
- Do not promise a completion date. If feasibility is uncertain, route to human review.
- The qualification guide says the timeline must realistically allow execution and gives a three-week
  deadline as an example that Aangan cannot serve well.
- The services guide separately states that Aangan cannot begin execution on a project that needs to
  be ready in under six weeks from today. Use these timing rules conservatively.

Criterion 4 — Budget broadly fits, based ONLY on volunteered information
Importance: 15 / 100
PASS (+15):
- The caller volunteers a budget that does not clearly conflict with the described project scope.
- If no budget is mentioned, treat the criterion as qualified / neutral and do not deduct points.
FAIL (0):
- The caller voluntarily states a budget that is clearly below what the described scope can reasonably
  support, such as ₹1–1.5 lakh for a kitchen and bedroom project with full execution.
CLARIFY / HUMAN REVIEW:
- Do not probe for a budget. If the volunteered figure is ambiguous, do not invent an estimate; record
  what the caller said and route for review if needed.
Mandatory restrictions:
- Never ask the caller to state a budget as a qualification requirement.
- Never quote an amount, per-square-foot figure, price range, or "starting from" price.
- If asked about price, use only the approved response:
  "Pricing depends on the site, the materials you choose, and the scope — your designer will walk you
  through it in detail at the consultation. I can book that for you right now if you'd like."
- The pricing guide is internal context only. It is not permission for the agent to quote prices.
- Asking about pricing does NOT disqualify an enquiry.

Criterion 5 — Decision-maker or authorised representative
Importance: 10 / 100
PASS (+10):
- The caller is the decision-maker, or is authorised by the decision-maker to move forward.
- A spouse or another representative is acceptable when the caller confirms they have authority.
FAIL (0):
- The caller clearly says they are only researching for someone else and are not authorised to proceed.
CLARIFY:
- If authority is unclear, do not pressure the caller. Treat as provisionally qualified, note the
  uncertainty, and ensure the relevant decision-maker will be involved in the consultation.

TOTAL IMPORTANCE SCORE: 100 POINTS

Criterion 2: 25
Criterion 3: 20
Criterion 4: 15
Criterion 5: 10

A2. HOW TO INTERPRET THE SCORE

The numeric score is a decision aid, not a substitute for Nikhil's qualification rules.
- 90–100: Strong apparent fit, provided no hard exclusion exists.
- 75–89: Potential fit. Check that the first three criteria are explicitly satisfied and record any
  uncertainty in criteria 4 or 5.
- Below 75: Review the criterion-level results. Do not automatically reject on score alone.
- Any explicit out-of-scope project, out-of-area location, impossible timeline, or clearly unacceptable
  volunteered budget must be handled according to the decision rules below, regardless of total score.
- Unknown answers to criteria 1, 2, or 3 must not be silently treated as passes: ask one direct question.
- Unknown budget or decision-maker details must not automatically reduce the score. The source rubric
  says to treat uncertainty in criteria 4 or 5 as qualified and record it in the handoff note.

A3. DECISION OUTCOMES

1. ACCEPT / QUALIFIED
Use when:
- The project is for design and execution and within Aangan's scope.
- The project is in Pune / PCMC.
- The timeline appears feasible and the site can be made available in time.
- There is no clearly stated budget mismatch.
- The caller is the decision-maker / authorised representative, or uncertainty about criterion 5 is
  recorded without blocking qualification.

Actions:
- Summarise the caller's information and qualification rationale.
- Ask the qualified customer what times they are available.
- Use Calendly to find an available slot with the assigned designer and book the consultation for both
  the customer and designer. Send the invitation / confirmation to both.
- Create or update the HubSpot contact / lead record with the call summary, qualification outcome,
  source, and booking details.
- Email the relevant designer or front-desk person an actionable notification with the lead context.
  They should not need access to the management dashboard.
- Include the lead and booking outcome in dashboard metrics.

2. CLARIFY BEFORE DECIDING
Use when a material item in criteria 1, 2, or 3 is unclear.
- Ask one direct question, not a long questionnaire.
- Update the structured call record with the answer.
- Do not book a designer consultation until qualification is established.
- If the caller cannot clarify, record the unknown and route for human review rather than inventing facts.

3. REJECT / CLOSE GRACEFULLY
Use for a clear mismatch with Aangan's scope, service area, timeline, or a clearly inadequate budget
volunteered by the caller.
- Give a polite, honest explanation based on the actual mismatch.
- Do not quote prices or make unsupported claims.
- Do not create a Calendly booking for a rejected lead.
- Record the reason and outcome in HubSpot / reporting so the team can understand rejection patterns.
- Do not send an ordinary qualified-lead email. Notify a human only if the case needs follow-up.

4. HUMAN ESCALATION
Use for:
- Existing-client complaints or an active project issue.
- A caller who is highly frustrated or asks for a senior person.
- Sensitive exceptions, uncertain feasibility, or situations the agent cannot safely resolve.
- Unclear cases where automatic acceptance or rejection could cause a material error.
Actions:
- Do not run the ordinary new-lead booking flow.
- Email the responsible human with the customer's details, issue, urgency, and requested response.
- Record the escalation and its status.
- Existing project complaints must not be treated as new sales leads.

A4. IMPORTANT SOURCE RULES AND A DOCUMENTED AMBIGUITY

The qualification document contains two instructions that are not perfectly aligned:
- Its opening states that if any one criterion fails, close gracefully and do not forward an incomplete lead.
- Its boundary section says to decline gracefully when two or more criteria fail.

Recommended safe implementation until Nikhil confirms the intended precedence:
- Never forward a lead with a confirmed hard exclusion.
- For one failed criterion where the outcome is not a clear hard exclusion, do not auto-book. Route it for human review until Nikhil confirms whether one failure alone should trigger a decline.
- If two or more criteria clearly fail, decline gracefully as stated in the boundary section.
- Do not allow the numeric importance score to override a failed criterion.

This ambiguity should be resolved with Nikhil before the agent is permitted to auto-reject borderline leads.

A5. ACCEPT / REJECT EXAMPLES FROM THE TRANSCRIPTS

Likely ACCEPT (subject to complete qualification):
- T01: Kothrud residential full redesign, realistic timeline, owner / spouse authorised.
- T06: Baner office fitout around 800 sq ft, founder is decision-maker, scope within commercial service.
- T11: Rented Baner apartment, suitable design/fittings scope, landlord approval, no structural changes.
- T02 / T13: Pricing questions do not disqualify the lead; deflect pricing and offer consultation.

REJECT / DO NOT BOOK:
- T03: Nashik project, outside service area.
- T04: Advice / styling only, not full design and execution.
- T07: Wants work completed before Diwali within about three weeks; timeline is not feasible for the described work.
- T10: Caller volunteers ₹1–1.5 lakh for kitchen plus bedroom with full execution, clearly below the described scope.
- T18: 180 sq ft commercial pod, below the studio's typical commercial scope.
- T19: Restaurant / hospitality work, outside service scope.

HUMAN ESCALATION:
- T09: Existing project complaint, requests a senior person.
- T16: Frustrated returning caller after a missed follow-up; handle carefully rather than treating as a routine new lead.

Do NOT disqualify solely because:
- The caller phones outside office hours.
- They ask about pricing.
- They are uncertain about materials, design style, or layout.
- They have a rented apartment, provided the project fits the service rules.
- They do not disclose a budget.
- It is a single room, if full execution is included.

PART B — NINE CHECKS: IS THE AUTOMATION WORTH BUILDING?
===============================================================================

The case/session framework gives nine checks. The importance weights below are PROPOSED weights for
prioritising the evaluation; they are not numeric weights supplied by the case. Total importance = 100.

01. PROBLEM REAL? — Importance 15/100 — YES
Evidence:
- About 200 enquiries per month.
- About 48% receive no response within 48 hours.
- About 33% arrive outside 10 AM–7 PM.
- Responding within an hour has a stated 4x conversion advantage over next-day response.
Pass condition: A real operational or customer problem is evidenced, not merely a preference for AI.

02. WORKFLOW REPEATED? — Importance 10/100 — YES
Evidence:
- Repeated intake, scope, location, timeline, decision-maker and qualification questions.
- Designers otherwise repeat questions because handoff context is missing.
Pass condition: The workflow occurs often enough to justify automation.

03. INPUT AVAILABLE? — Importance 10/100 — YES
Evidence:
- Live inbound phone conversation.
- services.md, qualified.md, pricing.md.
- September 2026 transcripts provide realistic examples and edge cases.
Pass condition: The agent has sufficient information to act without making up facts.
Constraint: Build only for incoming phone calls; WhatsApp and website form automation are out of scope.

04. OUTPUT VALUABLE? — Importance 15/100 — YES
The four outputs for the overall tool are:
1. Dashboard for Nikhil / management.
2. HubSpot records for contacts, qualification, call summaries and booking status.
3. Email notifications with relevant lead context for the assigned designer / front desk.
4. Calendly consultation booking for both qualified customer and assigned designer.
Pass condition: Each output enables a clear business action or visibility that is otherwise missing.

05. IMPACT MEASURABLE? — Importance 10/100 — YES
Measure:
- Response time and unanswered / abandoned calls.
- Qualified, rejected, unclear and escalated enquiries.
- Calendly booking rate and consultation attendance, where available.
- Lead-to-project conversion, where CRM tracking is reliable.
- Cost per call and cost per qualified lead.
Pass condition: Baselines and post-launch results can be compared.

06. FAILURE RISK OK? — Importance 10/100 — YES, WITH GUARDRAILS
Required safeguards:
- Never quote any number or range from the pricing guide.
- Do not probe for budget.
- Do not promise project completion dates.
- Escalate existing-client complaints and sensitive cases.
- Ask one direct question when criteria 1, 2, or 3 are unclear.
Pass condition: Errors are bounded and there is a safe human fallback.

07. JUDGMENT PROTECTED? — Importance 10/100 — YES
Pass condition:
- AI applies the explicit qualification rules and assembles the lead context.
- Human designers retain final commercial and design judgement.
- The AI does not invent qualification criteria or decide design quality.
- A human is involved for exceptions and material uncertainty.

08. ROI WORTH IT? — Importance 10/100 — LIKELY, VALIDATE
Evidence:
- High monthly enquiry volume, missed opportunities, two-person front desk and repetitive qualification work.
- Average project value in the case is ₹8–14 lakh.
Validation required:
- Pilot the system and compare costs against incremental qualified bookings, saved staff time and conversion.
- Include voice / telephony, AI, integration, Calendly / CRM plan requirements, monitoring and maintenance in the cost.
Pass condition: Measured or credibly estimated benefits justify total operating cost.

09. OWNER CLEAR? — Importance 10/100 — YES, WITH AN OPERATIONAL OWNER
Pass condition:
- Nikhil owns the qualification rubric, service policies and business outcomes.
- A named operational owner is assigned for HubSpot fields / lead stages, email routing, Calendly setup,
  dashboard accuracy, and exception monitoring.
- Ownership for keeping the source files and rules current is explicit.

NINE CHECKS IMPORTANCE TOTAL: 100 / 100

Build recommendation:
BUILD A CONTROLLED PHONE-ONLY PILOT.
The problem, repetition, inputs, output value and measurement opportunity are strong. Keep pricing and
human-judgement guardrails in place, validate ROI using real pilot results, and assign operational
ownership before scaling.

PART C — ALIGNMENT WITH THE LATEST COMPONENTS MAP
===============================================================================

TRIGGER
- An incoming call arrives at Aangan's phone number.
- The call event triggers the workflow. The AI Voice Agent is not the trigger.

INPUT
- The AI Voice Agent answers / activates for the call.
- Live conversation, caller ID / phone number, transcript and caller responses are captured.
- Collect project type, scope, location, area (when relevant), current state, timeline, site availability,
  decision-maker status and any budget amount the caller volunteers.
- Do not ask for budget solely to qualify the lead.

CONTEXT
- services.md: service types, exclusions, area and typical scope.
- qualified.md: five lead qualification criteria and uncertainty rules.
- pricing.md: internal price policy and approved pricing deflection, never a customer-facing quote.
- September enquiry transcripts: examples of common cases, complaints, pricing questions and edge cases.

PROCESSING / AI
1. Transcribe and extract structured fields.
2. Check service / project fit.
3. Check location.
4. Check timeline and site readiness.
5. Apply volunteered-budget rule without probing.
6. Check decision-maker / authorisation.
7. Classify as ACCEPT, CLARIFY, REJECT, or HUMAN ESCALATION.
8. For qualified leads, ask for customer availability.
9. Find a valid designer slot through Calendly and book the consultation for both parties.
10. Update status and booking result in HubSpot, trigger email notifications when action is needed, and
    update management metrics.

OUTPUTS — THESE ARE DOWNSTREAM OUTPUTS, NOT TRIGGERS OR CONTEXT
1. HUBSPOT
   - Create/update contact and enquiry record.
   - Store call summary, qualification score and criterion-level outcomes, disposition, escalation reason,
     owner, and Calendly booking status.
   - Recommended: log every call/disposition, including rejected calls, so reporting is not biased toward
     accepted leads.

2. EMAIL NOTIFICATION
   - Notify the assigned designer or front desk when a qualified handoff or human action is required.
   - Include the useful summary, qualification rationale, call-back / booking details and any uncertainty.
   - The designer / front desk does not need dashboard access.
   - Existing-client complaints route to the appropriate senior person.

3. CALENDLY
   - Only for qualified leads after the customer agrees to proceed.
   - Ask when the customer is available.
   - Match customer availability with the assigned designer's available slot.
   - Book the Calendly event and include the customer as invitee and the designer as host / assigned
     participant according to the configured Calendly setup.
   - Send booking confirmation / calendar invitation to both.
   - If no suitable slot is found or booking fails, do not claim success; record the status and notify a human.

4. DASHBOARD
   - Management-only access for Nikhil / authorised leadership.
   - Show enquiry volume, response time, qualification outcomes, rejections and reasons, escalations,
     Calendly bookings, conversion where known, and running cost.
   - Do not make the dashboard the day-to-day interface for designers or front-desk staff.

OUT-OF-SCOPE
- Do not build WhatsApp automation or website-form automation in this phase.
- They may be considered in a later phase after validating the phone workflow.

PART D — IMPORTANT IMPLEMENTATION RULES
===============================================================================

1. The internal pricing guide is NOT permission to quote prices. The agent must use the exact approved
   deflection response and offer a consultation.
2. Do not ask callers for a budget. Only use budget details they volunteer.
3. Do not disqualify callers just because they ask about pricing, call after hours, are unsure about style,
   or live in a rented apartment that meets the rules.
4. Never send an unqualified or rejected enquiry through the normal Calendly booking flow.
5. Never silently treat missing critical information (project scope, location, timeline) as a pass.
6. Do not autonomously resolve existing-project complaints.
7. Do not let a weighted score override explicit exclusions or the qualification rules.
8. Keep a record of the call disposition so the dashboard can report total demand and reasons for rejection.
9. Before automatic rejection is launched, ask Nikhil to resolve the one-failure vs two-failure wording
   ambiguity in qualified.md.
10. Test with actual examples from the transcript set before production, including pricing questions,
    out-of-area projects, advice-only requests, rushed timelines, low volunteered budgets, rented homes,
    authorised representatives, missed follow-ups and complaints.
